MCPs like USB ports (AI generated) There is a keyword that has recently taken the AI industry and developer communities by storm: MCP (Model Context Protocol) . Countless developers are busy setting up “MCP servers” locally, and AI agents are spreading their wings with this new tool. But for AI beginners or novice developers, terms like “protocol” and “server” still feel intimidating and foreign. “Can’t we just write good prompts? Why on earth do we need to spin up a server?” It’s a completely natural question. To summarize the core idea: An MCP server is a ‘universal outlet’ that seamlessly connects the brain of the AI (LLM) with all the data and tools of the world. While the smartphone revolution of the past birthed countless “standalone apps,” the AI agent revolution led by MCP is breaking down these individual apps, absorbing them as “Capabilities” of the AI. In this post, we will demystify the essence and background of the MCP server, explore why it sounds the death knell for traditional “app development” and “vibe-coding,” and deeply analyze what changes this forces upon the future Intent Architects . The Era of Chaotic AI Agents and the Birth of a Standard Q. How do AI agents read files on my computer and execute code? When we use Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT or Claude, they fundamentally think and answer within the boundaries of their pre-trained data. However, modern AI agents behave differently. They read files on your local machine, open browsers to search the web, and run queries on databases to fetch results. How is this possible? It’s thanks to “Tool Use” (or “Function Calling”) technology that allows AI to leverage external tools. Anthropic’s Strategic Gambit: MCP But there was a chronic bottleneck in this flow. If you wanted the AI to query a database, you had to write custom database query code and hand it over to the AI. If you wanted to integrate with GitHub, you had to write separate API call wrappers. If there were 100 different services to connect, developers had to manually build 100 entirely different connection bridges. Every specification was different, and interoperability was a nightmare. To resolve this inefficiency, in November 2024 , Anthropic (the creator of Claude) released a game-changing open-source standard: the Model Context Protocol (MCP) . Anthropic defined a standardized protocol to safely and consistently connect AI clients (like Claude Desktop) to where the actual data resides (like your local notes, databases, GitHub, etc.). The entity that serves this data in compliance with this standardized protocol is what we call an “MCP Server.” Deep Dive!
